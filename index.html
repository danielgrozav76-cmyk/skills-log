About this portfolio

I am a Level 3 business student at Ravensbourne University London. This webpage forms Part 3 of BSM24001 Critical and Creative Thinking. It evidences the core employability skills I attained across the module and reflects critically on how I developed them.

The module ran through three connected tasks: a multiple-choice quiz on critical thinking and research methods, a 20-minute interdisciplinary group debate on ethical and sustainability issues in the creative industries, and this portfolio. Each task is used below as concrete evidence for a named skill, rather than a general claim.

02

Core employability skills

Analytical competence

Critical thinking & logical reasoning

Applying structured reasoning, evaluating arguments, and distinguishing strong evidence from weak claims.

Evidence: Part 1 quiz on critical-thinking methods, logical reasoning and ethical frameworks, where I tested and sharpened how I assess arguments under timed conditions.
Information literacy

Research skills

Locating, judging and referencing credible academic sources to support a position.

Evidence: Sourcing debate material through JSTOR, Google Scholar and Emerald Insight, and citing it with Harvard referencing throughout my preparation materials.
Collaboration

Teamwork & collaboration

Contributing reliably within a mixed team, sharing workload and negotiating a shared line of argument.

Evidence: Working in an interdisciplinary group of 4–5 to plan and deliver the structured debate, dividing research and rehearsing together.
Communication

Communication & public speaking

Presenting clearly and professionally, structuring an argument and responding to counterpoints in real time.

Evidence: Delivering opening, supporting and closing statements in a 20-minute debate that simulated a professional setting, supported by visual aids.
Creativity

Creative thinking & innovation

Generating original angles and alternative solutions rather than accepting the obvious answer.

Evidence: Developing creative perspectives and fresh counter-arguments for the debate, and designing and building this portfolio from scratch.
Professional values

Ethical & sustainability awareness

Recognising ethical and sustainability implications and weighing them when forming a position.

Evidence: Debating ethical and sustainability issues within the creative industries (LO3), which required balancing commercial, social and environmental concerns.
Digital literacy

Digital & technical skills

Using digital tools to produce and publish professional work for an audience.

Evidence: Building this webpage and publishing it on GitHub Pages as a live, shareable portfolio for Part 3.
Self-management

Time management & professionalism

Planning across competing deadlines and meeting submission standards consistently.

Evidence: Managing three staggered deadlines (quiz, debate and portfolio) within Weeks 1–4 while keeping each submission to the required standard.

Reflective summary

Gibbs' Reflective Cycle (1988)
DescriptionFeelingsEvaluationAnalysisConclusionAction plan

Over BSM24001, I completed a quiz, a group debate, and this digital portfolio. Describing the experience, the interdisciplinary debate on sustainability in the creative industries demanded the most from me. At first I felt nervous about speaking for twenty minutes and relying on teammates I barely knew. Evaluating it, the debate went well because we divided research clearly, though our rebuttals were weaker than our opening. Analysing why, I realise structured preparation and Harvard-sourced evidence built my confidence, while uneven time management weakened later arguments. Succi and Canovi (2020) confirm that soft skills like teamwork and communication matter as much as knowledge to employers. I conclude that I strengthened critical thinking, collaboration, and public speaking, but must manage preparation time better. My action plan is to rehearse rebuttals earlier and lead a group task next semester to develop leadership alongside the competencies this module helped me evidence.
